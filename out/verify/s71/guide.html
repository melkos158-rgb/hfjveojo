Virtual staging styles: one room in six looks

Short answer: if you aren't sure, pick modern or Scandinavian. Both are neutral and light, and they suit most homes. Match farmhouse, mid-century and coastal to the home's age and location. Save luxury for listings where premium finishes are the point. That is our recommendation, not a rule. Below is the same empty living room staged in all six styles, so you can compare them side by side.

The room before staging

A living room with a white fireplace mantel, a wall of tall windows, a ceiling fan and a dark wood floor. In every version below, those stay exactly as photographed. Virtual staging adds furniture, rugs, lamps, plants and art; it doesn't move walls or swap fixtures.

The six styles

1. Modern

What defines it: Clean lines, a neutral base of white, cream and gray, black or metal accents, low and simple furniture, little ornament.

Good fit for: Condos, new construction and most mid-priced homes. The safest default when you don't know who the buyer will be.

2. Scandinavian

What defines it: Light woods, soft whites and creams, rounded shapes, natural textures like wool, linen and jute, and plants: airy and cozy at once.

Good fit for: Small or bright rooms, apartments and starter homes. Light, low furniture keeps a small room feeling open.

3. Farmhouse

What defines it: Warm rustic wood, comfortable rolled-arm or slipcovered seating, plaid and woven textures, vintage-style art.

Good fit for: Suburban and rural single-family homes, especially ones with traditional details: a mantel, beams, wide-plank floors.

4. Mid-century

What defines it: Walnut and teak tones, tapered legs, low profiles, organic shapes, bold accents in orange, mustard or olive, graphic art.

Good fit for: Homes from the 1950s and 60s, ranches, and design-minded city buyers.

5. Luxury

What defines it: Richer materials and more layers: deeper woods, plush textiles, statement lamps and art, a restrained palette with dark accents.

Good fit for: Higher-priced listings where finishes and lifestyle are the selling point. Ours leans to warm, quiet luxury rather than gold and glam.

6. Coastal

What defines it: Whites and sand tones with soft blues, slipcovered seating, rattan and wicker, pale woods, sea and shore art.

Good fit for: Beach and lake markets and vacation homes. Inland, it can look out of place.

How to choose a style for a listing

Our recommendation, in the order we'd think about it:

  1. Start from the house. Its age and architecture narrow the choice: a 1960s ranch takes mid-century naturally, a new build takes modern, and a traditional home with a mantel and trim takes farmhouse or luxury, depending on the price.
  2. Think about the likely buyer. First-time buyers and renters-turned-owners respond to light, practical rooms (Scandinavian, modern); move-up and high-end buyers expect more layers (luxury).
  3. Respect the location. Coastal belongs near water. Elsewhere, choose a neutral style.
  4. Work with what stays. Floors, wall colors and the fireplace don't change. Strong wall colors and busy floors call for calm, neutral furniture.
  5. Use one style for the whole listing. Rooms in different styles look like a catalog, not a home. Start with the rooms that matter most — see which rooms to stage first.
